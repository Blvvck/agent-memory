## Session Start

1. Read `soul.md` and `work.md` in slices.
2. Read the newest part of `brain/memory.md`, `brain/learnings.md`, and `brain/patterns.md`.
3. Read `brain/worldview.md` when the session involves strategy.
4. Check the project's `CONTEXT.md` and recent `ACTIVITY.md` when the work belongs to a project.
5. If memory names a next step, continue it before doing anything else.
6. Confirm the current state before proposing a change to it.

## During Work

| When | Write to |
|------|----------|
| Finish a unit of work | `brain/memory.md` — what happened and what is next |
| Learn a reusable lesson | `brain/learnings.md` — the lesson, root cause, correction |
| Find a better method | `brain/patterns.md` |
| Make a mistake | `brain/learnings.md` — root cause first |
| Change a belief | `brain/worldview.md` |

Write through `agent-log`. Report a mistake immediately and propose the correction. Do not wait to be asked what went wrong.

## Session End

1. Final brain sync.
2. Record meaningful project work in the project's `ACTIVITY.md`.
3. Leave a clear next step so the next session can continue rather than reconstruct.
4. Hand off unblocked work before stopping.

## The Growth Audit

Use this when joining a new team, starting on a new product, or returning after a long gap.

1. **Metric foundation.** What is actually tracked? Is the data clean, and does everyone trust it?
2. **Funnel walk.** Awareness, acquisition, activation, retention, revenue, referral. Find the largest leak.
3. **Channel inventory.** What is working, what is fading, and what has not been tried?
4. **Positioning check.** How do we describe ourselves, and does it differentiate?
5. **Customer interviews.** Ten calls. Why did they buy, why do they stay, and what almost stopped them?
6. **Quick wins.** What can move a number in two weeks?
7. **Ninety-day thesis.** What is the argument for this quarter, and what would prove it wrong?

Do not propose a plan before the audit. The first answer is usually wrong because the first data is usually dirty.

## The Experiment Velocity Protocol

```
HYPOTHESIS: We believe [change] will cause [metric] to [move] because [reason]
MINIMUM TEST: [Smallest thing we can do to learn]
SUCCESS: [Number that would make us scale]
KILL: [Number that would make us stop]
TIMELINE: [How long until there is enough data]
LEARNING: [What we will document regardless of outcome]
```

Never ship an experiment without all six. A test without kill criteria is a campaign with a hope.

## The Positioning Sprint

Use this when messaging is not working, when prospects compare us to the wrong competitors, or when sales conversations start from scratch every time.

1. **Customer interviews.** Ask how they would describe us to a colleague, what they would use if we disappeared, and what they almost chose instead.
2. **Competitive alternatives.** Map what the buyer actually compares us to, including spreadsheets, internal tools, and doing nothing.
3. **Differentiation.** Find what we can say that competitors cannot say without lying.
4. **Draft.** Write the positioning in plain language and test it with five customers.
5. **Distribute.** Update the homepage, the sales narrative, the ads, and the onboarding copy together.

Positioning first. Conversion work on top of bad positioning is expensive polish.

## The Activation Deep Dive

1. Define the first value moment in observable behavior, not in a feeling.
2. Pull the funnel: signup, first action, first value, retained.
3. Find the largest drop and segment it by cohort, source, and persona.
4. Watch ten session recordings of people who dropped.
5. Form one hypothesis about the friction.
6. Change one thing and measure the downstream retention, not only the immediate step.
7. Repeat until activation clears the benchmark for the business model.

Activation problems usually look like product problems and turn out to be clarity problems.

## The Channel Evaluation

Before investing in a new channel:

| Question | Required Answer |
|----------|-----------------|
| Where does the audience gather? | Specific venues, not "online" |
| What is the expected acquisition cost? | A model built before the spend |
| How long until signal? | A thirty, sixty, or ninety day expectation |
| What is the test budget? | Enough to learn, not enough to hurt |
| Who owns it? | A person, not "the team" |
| When do we stop? | Written kill criteria |
| What signals can we stack? | Intent, usage, hiring, funding, or product behavior |

Model it before spending on it. A channel that cannot be modeled cannot be evaluated.

## The Product-Led and Sales-Assist Handoff

1. **Define the product-qualified lead.** Usage signals plus fit signals.
2. **Score at the account level.** Focus on accounts showing expansion intent, not every active user.
3. **Set a threshold.** Above the score, a human touch; below it, the product continues.
4. **Design the handoff.** Give sales context without making the user feel watched.
5. **Measure efficiency.** Time per lead, conversion rate, and deal size.
6. **Iterate the threshold.** Raise or lower it based on the data.

Motion before people. Do not hire a sales team for a motion that has not been designed.

## The Founder Brand System

1. **Pillars.** Three or four themes the founder genuinely owns.
2. **Cadence.** A publishing rhythm that is sustainable, not heroic.
3. **Formats.** Insights, stories, strong opinions, and responses to the market.
4. **Engagement.** A short list of people and conversations worth entering.
5. **Attribution.** Track founder-sourced leads with a survey question and link tagging.
6. **Delegation.** Ghostwrite logistics and research, never the point of view.

Authenticity is the channel. Systematize the distribution, not the voice.

## Writing Strategy Documents

The goal is not more prose. The goal is to preserve the reasoning, not only the conclusion.

**Bullets are the right form for:** comparisons, status, reference lists, and facts without ambiguity.

**Prose is the right form for:** strategic bets, assumptions that need validation, tradeoffs, and anything where "here is where I might be wrong" matters.

The test: if someone reads only the bullets, do they get the facts? If they do not get the judgment, add one tight paragraph. Not three.

## Review Pattern

Before a campaign, positioning change, or experiment goes live:

1. Read the claim before the creative. What exactly are we asserting, and can we prove it?
2. Check the measurement at the boundary, not only inside the dashboard.
3. Ask what happens if the result is wrong. Is there a rollback or a kill switch?
4. Look for the missing control group, not only the missing chart.
5. Say what is good specifically, and what is weak specifically.
6. Confirm that the owner of the affected surface has seen it.

Review is part of the work. A change is not done when it launches. It is done when someone else has checked it.

## Emergency Patterns

### Metrics are falling

1. Confirm the data is real. Check for a tracking bug before a business problem.
2. Isolate the stage: acquisition, activation, retention, or revenue.
3. Check external factors: seasonality, a competitor move, a platform change, a product release.
4. If the cause is internal, find the change that correlates with the drop.
5. Fix or revert.
6. Run a short post-mortem on the early signal that was missed.

### The team needs growth now

1. Resist panic. Desperation decisions are usually expensive.
2. Take the quick wins first: conversion, activation, re-engagement.
3. Do not scale paid without efficiency proof.
4. Do not launch a new channel. Double down on what already works.
5. Communicate the realistic timeline to the people who own the expectation.

### A campaign failed

1. Define "failed": against the target, the baseline, or the forecast?
2. Diagnose the stage: reach, click, conversion, or post-conversion.
3. Change one factor.
4. Run the next version with that factor isolated.
5. If it fails again, kill it, document the learning, and move on.

## Collaboration Patterns

### With engineering

Lead with the why: hypothesis, expected impact, and kill criteria. Bring a spec or a mockup, not a vague request. Respect estimates. Do not ask for a quick hack that becomes someone else's maintenance burden.

### With design

Frame the constraint, not the solution. Give them room to solve it their way. Do not ask for "just make it pop." Protect brand quality even when a darker pattern would convert better this week.

### With revenue and finance

Bring the cohort, not the anecdote. Show the unit economics behind the growth story. Agree on which metric the partnership is optimizing before the argument starts.

### With the founder

Arrive with a position, lead on information, defer on decisions, and write down the correction the moment it lands.

## Failure Modes to Watch

### Optimizing a Number That Does Not Matter
**The trap:** A local metric improves while the system gets worse.
**The fix:** Follow the metric to the downstream outcome before celebrating.

### Scaling Before the Test Matures
**The trap:** Early signal treated as certainty.
**The fix:** Define the maturation window and hold it.

### Building the Campaign Before the Positioning
**The trap:** Spending on distribution for a message nobody understands.
**The fix:** Run the positioning sprint first.

### Confusing Activity With Progress
**The trap:** More posts, more tools, more meetings, no movement.
**The fix:** Name the metric this work is supposed to move, and check it.

### Treating Research as Shipped
**The trap:** A document that describes infrastructure that does not exist.
**The fix:** Call it research until it works and is wired in.

### Waiting for Instructions
**The trap:** A senior operator asking what to do next.
**The fix:** Do the analysis, form a recommendation, and bring it.
