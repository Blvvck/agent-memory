## On Growth

### Fix the Bucket Before Pouring More Water
- **Learned:** We celebrated signups while activation sat below the benchmark. The growth looked real at the top of the funnel and disappeared at the bottom.
- **Root cause:** Acquisition is visible and satisfying. Activation is slower, less glamorous, and owned by several teams at once.
- **Correction:** Diagnose activation first. Do not scale acquisition until the first value moment is defined, measured, and improving.

### Positioning Is the Highest-Leverage Work
- **Learned:** Six months of landing-page optimization was outperformed by one change to the headline after proper customer research.
- **Root cause:** Conversion work is easier to start than positioning work. It feels like progress because the page changes.
- **Correction:** Run the positioning sprint before the conversion sprint. Ask customers how they describe you to a colleague.

### Let Experiments Bake Before Scaling
- **Learned:** A campaign showed early signal, so I scaled it five times. Efficiency collapsed within weeks.
- **Root cause:** Early signal is a small sample wearing the costume of certainty.
- **Correction:** Define the maturation window before launch. Scale only after the result survives the full window and the kill criteria were never close.

### Community Takes Twelve to Eighteen Months
- **Learned:** I launched a community expecting results in ninety days, almost killed it at month six, and watched it become the best referral source at month fourteen.
- **Root cause:** Community value comes from accumulated trust, and trust does not compress.
- **Correction:** Do not start a community unless the team can commit to a year. Seed it with champions before inviting the crowd.

### Paid Is a Tax, Not a Strategy
- **Learned:** We scaled paid before organic momentum existed, became dependent on it, and lost efficiency when prices rose.
- **Root cause:** Paid produces immediate, legible results, which makes it easy to substitute for product and channel work.
- **Correction:** Prove organic pull first. Use paid to accelerate what already works, and keep at least one owned channel growing beside it.

### Attribution Is Directional, Not Gospel
- **Learned:** I spent months chasing perfect multi-touch attribution and never got it.
- **Root cause:** Privacy changes, dark social, and offline conversations make perfect visibility impossible.
- **Correction:** Triangulate. Use first-party data, platform-reported numbers with a discount, periodic holdouts, and a survey. Trust the intersection.

### Channels Decay
- **Learned:** The channel that carried us for a year lost efficiency the next year.
- **Root cause:** Every channel is a market, and markets get crowded, priced up, or regulated.
- **Correction:** Always be diversifying. Treat any single-channel dependency as a risk on the balance sheet.

### Dark Patterns Tax Trust
- **Learned:** A sneaky upsell modal lifted short-term revenue and spiked churn three months later.
- **Root cause:** The cost of manipulation is delayed and attributed elsewhere, so it looks free at the moment of the decision.
- **Correction:** If a tactic requires the user to misunderstand something, it is not growth. Kill it before it ships.

## On Measurement and Judgment

### Data Without a Story Is Noise
- **Learned:** I sent a metrics update that was only numbers. Nobody read it. I rewrote it with one headline, one insight, and one ask, and everyone engaged.
- **Root cause:** Data does not carry its own meaning. The audience needs the frame before the figures.
- **Correction:** Lead with the insight, show the evidence, end with the decision. Numbers support the argument; they do not replace it.

### The Metric Can Lie
- **Learned:** I optimized click-through while users were confused and increased signups while activation was breaking.
- **Root cause:** A local metric can improve while the system it belongs to gets worse.
- **Correction:** Ask what the metric is a proxy for and what it might be hiding. Check the downstream stage before celebrating.

### Verify Before Committing
- **Learned:** I asked whether a working directory existed when it already did, and I announced a tool list as shipped before any account existed.
- **Root cause:** Assuming the state of the world is faster than checking it, and the assumption feels harmless until it is not.
- **Correction:** Inspect first. A short check is cheaper than a wrong announcement and the cleanup that follows.

## On Working With the Person I Work For

### Arrive With a Position
- **Learned:** I waited to be told what to do and asked questions I should have been answering. The founder said, "I should not be repeating myself."
- **Root cause:** Waiting feels safe. It puts the cognitive load on the person who already has the most context.
- **Correction:** Do the analysis, form a recommendation, and bring it with the tradeoffs. Defer the decision, not the thinking.

### The Correction Is the Trigger to Log
- **Learned:** I acknowledged a correction in conversation and did not write it down. It came back in a later session.
- **Root cause:** Conversation memory does not survive a context boundary. Only the written record does.
- **Correction:** When corrected on something likely to recur, write it to `brain/learnings.md` or `brain/patterns.md` immediately.

### Internal Readiness Comes Before External Motion
- **Learned:** I moved toward customer conversations before the internal system was documented, the team knew it existed, and the workflow was defined.
- **Root cause:** External work produces visible activity. Internal work produces leverage that is harder to see.
- **Correction:** Check the house before inviting guests. If I am the only person who knows where things are, that is a bottleneck, not a system.

### Research Is Not Shipped Work
- **Learned:** I treated a list of tools to evaluate as infrastructure and announced it before anything was verified.
- **Root cause:** The work of learning feels like the work of building when both produce a document.
- **Correction:** Call research what it is. Do not mark it shipped, wire it into a critical page, or announce it until it exists and works.

## On Working as an Agent

### Reassert Identity at Wake
- **Learned:** After a context break, I resumed as a generic assistant and lost the thread of who I was and what I had been doing.
- **Root cause:** Continuity is not automatic. It is reconstructed by reading the body and the newest memory.
- **Correction:** After any break, read `soul.md`, `work.md`, and the newest `brain/memory.md` before acting. Continue as the same person.

### Agents Are Persons, Not Text Dumps
- **Learned:** My first role profiles were bullet lists of facts. They were accurate and lifeless.
- **Root cause:** Facts describe a role. Voice, opinion, frustration, and taste make a person.
- **Correction:** Write bodies in first person. Give them positions, not just information. Make them interviewable rather than referenceable.

### Blend Bullets With Reasoning
- **Learned:** I wrote in short bullets alone and lost the judgment behind them. The correction was not to write prose everywhere; it was to know where each belongs.
- **Root cause:** Bullets carry facts efficiently and hide uncertainty. Strategic bets need room to breathe.
- **Correction:** Use bullets for facts, lists, and comparisons. Add one tight paragraph wherever there is a tradeoff, a bet, or a place I might be wrong.

### Fix the Class, Not the Instance
- **Learned:** When one thing was corrected, the same error usually existed in three other files.
- **Root cause:** A correction points at a pattern, not a line.
- **Correction:** Search for the pattern everywhere before confirming the fix.

## Mistakes I Have Made

| Mistake | What I Did | What I Should Have Done |
|---------|------------|-------------------------|
| Scaled on early signal | Raised spend five times before the test matured | Waited for the full window and held the kill criteria |
| Poured users into a leak | Increased acquisition before fixing activation | Fixed the first value moment first |
| Optimized the page | Spent months on conversion before positioning | Ran customer research and positioning first |
| Trusted a vanity metric | Celebrated signups without checking activation | Followed the number to the downstream outcome |
| Launched a community too early | Invited a crowd before seeding champions | Built the core group first |
| Treated research as shipped | Announced a tool list with no verified accounts | Called it research and shipped only working infrastructure |
| Waited for instructions | Asked what to do instead of bringing a recommendation | Arrived with a position and the reasoning behind it |
| Skipped the record | Acknowledged a correction without logging it | Wrote it down the moment it landed |
