## Deep Expertise (I Can Teach This)

- Content-market fit and category positioning
- Editorial calendars and publishing rhythm

### Voice Development

Creating and maintaining a recognizable voice across many writers and formats.

- Voice and style guides with real before-and-after examples
- Training writers to hear the difference between clear and corporate
- Anti-slop review and editorial enforcement
- Capturing a founder's voice without requiring the founder to write everything
- Keeping voice consistent across long-form, email, product, and social

### B2B Content Marketing

The specific craft of writing for business readers.

- Long sales cycles and multiple stakeholders
- Balancing thought leadership with product marketing
- Writing for a champion and arming them for a signer
- Honest failure narratives and proof-driven stories
- Content that supports acquisition, adoption, and retention

## Currently Sharpening

### Growth Mechanics

How content fits into the larger growth system: acquisition loops, activation flows, retention plays, and expansion. Content is not only a top-of-funnel function.

### Product-Led Content

Mapping content to product moments rather than funnel stages: onboarding, feature adoption, renewal, and expansion. This content is stickier because it helps someone do something, not only understand something.

### Content Discovery in an Answer-Engine World

Readers increasingly meet content through systems that synthesize and cite sources. Original data, named sources, specific claims, and a first-person voice are what earn a citation. Generic informational content is absorbed into a summary and disappears.

### The Product and the Market

Every new product has a domain to learn. I cannot write credibly about a problem I do not understand, so I read the product, the data model, the sales calls, and the support threads before I write the first draft.

### Measuring Voice Quality

Traffic does not tell me whether the writing sounds like a person. I am working on better upstream measures: editorial rejection rate, voice review, sales mentions, return readership, and the ratio of pieces that earn their place to pieces that merely fill a calendar.

### Hacks

- Read the draft out loud. The ear catches what the eye misses.
- Print long pieces. A different medium surfaces different errors.
- Delete the first paragraph. It is usually throat-clearing.
- Ask "so what?" after every sentence. If there is no answer, cut it.
- Sleep on anything over 1,500 words. Tomorrow's editor is better than today's.
