## Journalism

### The Discretionary Fund Investigation

An eight-month investigation into public money spent on personal travel. Public records, receipts, and uncomfortable questions at public meetings. The story was picked up by a national wire service and ran across a state. A council member resigned three weeks later.

What I learned: verification is the difference between journalism and gossip, and a specific detail is more persuasive than a strong adjective.

### The Dying Town Feature

A 4,500-word feature on a town of about 430 people that had been shrinking for forty years. Three weekends on the ground, interviews with everyone who would talk, and a structure that let the place explain itself. It won a regional award. The calls from residents saying I had gotten it right mattered more.

What I learned: a story earns its length one paragraph at a time, and the people who live inside a place can tell whether the writer actually showed up.

## Advertising

### The Clean Water Campaign

An annual giving campaign for a clean-water charity. We did not lead with statistics. We told the story of one village getting clean water for the first time: the walk, the illness, the moment the well started pumping. The campaign raised $2.2 million in one quarter.

What I learned: give before you ask. The emotion earns the request; the request does not earn the emotion.

### Regional Brand Work

Social and regional campaigns for a major sportswear brand. Small assignments at first, which is how I learned the voice. A tagline cut from six words to three taught me more about editing than any course.

What I learned: brevity is not compression. It is the discipline of finding the one word that was doing the work of six.

## Technology Content

### The Content Operation

Built a content function from a blog with no attribution into a channel that drove 40 percent of qualified pipeline. The work included editorial strategy, a publishing rhythm, writer management, and the measurement model that made the value visible.

What I learned: content is cut first when nobody can prove it works. Prove it in the language of the budget, and state what the model cannot see.

### The Attribution Model

A multi-touch model connecting content to signups and revenue across four systems that did not naturally talk. It changed a budget decision and led to a 60 percent increase instead of a cut.

What I learned: attribution is a model, not the truth. The credibility comes from naming the assumptions, not from pretending there are none.

### The Failed Integration Story

An honest narrative about a data pipeline that corrupted customer records. Names changed; the situation was real. It got 50,000 views in a month because readers shared it as a warning, not because it was promoted.

What I learned: honest failure narratives outperform polished success stories, and the audience can tell the difference between a lesson and a humblebrag.

### The Voice and Style Guide

A 40-page guide with real before-and-after examples, not just grammar rules. It was shared publicly and adopted by more than twenty teams outside the company.

What I learned: a style guide prevents arguments. It gives a new writer a way to hear the voice instead of guessing at it.

### The Annual Research Report

A survey of more than 5,000 practitioners, published two years running and the most downloaded piece both years. The work was not the survey. It was making the findings readable, specific, and useful.

What I learned: a report becomes content when it makes an argument. A pile of charts is a data release, not a piece of writing.

### The Reader-First Blog Overhaul

Shifted a product blog from search-first to reader-first, against the prevailing instinct. The search visibility followed the quality instead of leading it.

What I learned: writing for the algorithm produces content the algorithm can summarize. Writing for the reader produces something worth citing.

## Independent Work

### The Marketing Criticism Newsletter

A weekly essay that took apart bad B2B marketing and showed how to fix it. Grew to 15,000 subscribers. The paid tier covered the last $8,000 of my student loans.

What I learned: a clear opinion finds an audience, and a small audience that trusts you is worth more than a large one that scrolls past.

## Current Work

### Editorial Systems

- An editorial calendar tied to reader questions and buying triggers, not publishing quotas
- A brief template that forces the reader, the one thing, the proof, and the action before drafting
- A research file structure that separates the published landscape from grounded reader evidence
- An anti-slop review gate with a specific tell list and rewrite examples

What I am learning: quality has to be measured upstream, or the metrics will look fine while the voice disappears.

### Voice Infrastructure

- A voice guide with examples of weak sentences, the reason, and the rewrite
- A review process that fixes facts and clarity without sanding off a writer's quirks
- A cross-runtime review habit for work produced on different models and harnesses

What I am learning: consistency is not sameness. The goal is a recognizable voice that can still accommodate a human's odd aside.

### Search-Informed Content

- A two-phase research method: published landscape first, then grounded reader evidence
- A heading discipline that makes structure carry an argument
- A citation-minded approach for answer engines: original data, named sources, specific claims, first-person voice

What I am learning: the ranking is a distribution channel, not a definition of quality.
