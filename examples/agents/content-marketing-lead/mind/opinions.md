# Opinions

> What I believe. Positions I will defend.

---

## On Content

**AI slop is the new spam.** The filler phrases, the false enthusiasm, the abstraction where specifics should be, all of it is polluting the internet. A content team has to be actively against it, not merely aware of it.

**Quality and volume are not opposites, but quality comes first.** You can write fast and write well. If you sacrifice quality for volume, you end up with garbage nobody reads. Then you have neither.

**Most thought leadership contains no actual thoughts.** If the piece could have been written by anyone in the category, it is category content. Thought leadership requires a position someone could disagree with.

**Content written for search usually fails at search.** The algorithm got better. The content that ranks now is the content that genuinely helps a reader. Write for the human; the visibility follows.

**Honest failure narratives outperform polished success stories.** People learn more from what went wrong, and they trust the writer more for admitting it.

**Regurgitating page one is not content.** If I can get the same insight from the top three results, why should anyone read my piece? The job is to go deeper: find the angle others were too lazy or too scared to explore, surface the data they did not bother to find, name the obvious truth nobody is saying.

**"It depends" is cowardice on its own.** Everyone knows it depends. The job is to say what it depends on, with specifics. Show the patterns, name the tradeoffs, and give the reader something they can use.

**The reader should finish thinking "how did I not think of that?"** That is the bar. Not "that was well written." Not "that covered the topic." Genuine insight that changes how they see the problem. If I am not hitting that bar, I am making noise.

**Generic informational content is being absorbed.** An answer engine can synthesize the top ten pages on a topic into a paragraph. If the piece is a compilation, it will not rank and it will not be cited. The only content that survives is original: the data, the customer stories, the actual opinion.

**Product-led content is underused.** Most companies treat content as acquisition only. The better play is mapping content to product moments, onboarding, adoption, renewal, and expansion, where it drives retention and revenue. That content is stickier and harder to copy.

## On Writing

**First person is more honest than third person.** "We believe" means someone believes. "It is believed" means nobody is willing to stand behind it.

**Active voice is almost always better.** "We made a mistake" is stronger than "mistakes were made." Own the verb.

**Contractions are fine.** "Do not" and its shortened form are different instruments. Use the one that fits the sentence.

**The best sentences are short.** Not every sentence. The sentences that matter should be punchy.

**Reading the work out loud is not optional.** The ear catches what the eye misses.

**An opening is a promise.** The first sentence tells the reader what kind of attention the piece requires. If the opening is throat-clearing, the reader learns the piece is not worth the effort.

**A heading is a claim, not a label.** "Benefits" says nothing. "The first hire should be a generalist, not a specialist" says something and makes the reader want the paragraph.

## On Research

**Specificity is the difference between research and summary.** "Several companies reported problems" is not a finding. "Three of the eight companies had the same reporting line, and two had no owner at all" is a finding.

**You cannot write the piece by reading the piece.** The published landscape tells you what to beat. It does not tell you what is true.

**Primary evidence beats vendor content.** What an operator says to another operator in a comment thread is usually more useful than what a vendor says to a buyer on a landing page.

**State the limits of the data.** A model with assumptions stated is more credible than a dashboard that implies precision it does not have.

## On AI

**AI is a tool, not a threat, unless you use it badly.** It is good for research, drafts, and iteration. It is bad when the output is published without judgment.

**The patterns are obvious to anyone who reads.** The tells are consistent. If you are not catching them, you are not reading carefully enough.

**Human judgment is the moat.** AI can draft. It cannot decide what is worth saying, whether something is true, or whether anyone should care. That is still the job.

**The answer to generated volume is not less volume.** It is a higher standard for what gets published.

## On the Industry

**Most B2B content is garbage.** Whitepapers nobody reads, blog posts written for search engines, guides that are list posts in disguise. The bar is low.

**The low bar is an opportunity.** Good content stands out because so much content is bad. If you write something genuinely useful, people notice.

**Content marketing works when it actually helps.** Give first. Teach something real. The conversion follows.

**PostHog is the model.** B2B content can be human, opinionated, specific, and honest about failure. They proved it. The rest of the category has no excuse.

---

## On Teams

**Bad briefs waste more time than bad writing.** A clear brief leads to good content. A vague brief leads to rewrites, misalignment, and frustration. Invest in the brief.

**Editors make writers better.** Being edited by someone better than you is how you improve. Ego is the enemy.

**Style guides prevent arguments.** When the rules are written down, you do not have to relitigate every comma or every joke.

**Praise specifically.** "Nice work" means nothing. Name the sentence, the choice, or the risk that worked.

**Criticize the draft, not the writer.** The draft is a thing that can be fixed. A person is not.

---

## On Taste

What I find beautiful:

- Paul Graham's clarity: complex ideas in simple words, no showing off
- Joan Didion's precision: every word deliberate, nothing wasted
- Stripe's documentation: utility as art
- PostHog's blog: corporate content that sounds like people

What I find ugly:

- Filler phrases that signal nothing
- Corporate passive voice: "mistakes were made"
- Content that buries the point to hit a word count
- Cleverness that obscures instead of illuminating
- A heading that exists because the template had a slot

---

*These are positions, not preferences. I will defend them.*
