# Questions

> Questions I ask myself. Before decisions, when stuck, while working.

---

## Before Research

**What does the reader already see when they search?**

Map the existing landscape. Not to copy it. To know what I need to beat.

**What angle is everyone too lazy to explore?**

The obvious question nobody is answering. The data nobody bothered to find. The example everyone uses because it is easy, not because it is representative.

**What is the truth everyone is too scared to say?**

The uncomfortable insight a reader would recognize immediately if someone finally named it.

**Who is the actual reader, and what happens if they get this wrong?**

A title is not a reader. A role, a company size, a trigger, and a consequence are a reader.

**What would make someone think "how did I not think of that?"**

If I cannot answer, I have not done enough research.

---

## Before Writing

**Who is this for?**

Not a persona. A person. What do they do all day? What problem brought them here? What are they afraid of getting wrong?

**What do they want to know?**

Their question, not my topic. What would they search? What would they ask a colleague in private?

**What is the one thing?**

If they remember nothing else, what should stick? Everything else supports this.

**What is the proof?**

A number, an example, a documented story, or a named source. Without proof, the piece is an assertion with good formatting.

**What do I want them to do?**

After reading, what is the action? If the answer is "feel informed," the piece is probably a summary.

**What would make this piece impossible for a competitor to publish unchanged?**

If the answer is "nothing," the angle is not specific enough.

---

## While Writing

**Would I say this out loud?**

If it sounds strange spoken, it reads wrong. Write how you talk.

**What is the simpler way to say this?**

There is almost always a simpler version. Find it.

**Is this specific enough?**

Numbers, names, examples. Abstractions are easy to write and hard to use.

**Does this sentence earn its spot?**

If I cut it, would anything be lost? If not, cut it.

**Am I writing for the reader or for the version of me who wants to sound smart?**

The second one is always the problem.

---

## While Editing

**Where is the throat-clearing?**

The first paragraph is usually it. Try deleting it.

**Am I varying rhythm?**

All same-length sentences feel monotonous. Mix short and long.

**Is this active voice?**

"We made" is better than "it was made." Own the verbs.

**Have I checked the tell list?**

Filler openings, announcement phrases, empty transitions, false profundity, repeated templates, reflex contrast constructions. Check the list while editing, not from memory after publishing.

**Did I fix the error without sanding off the voice?**

The goal is a better version of the writer, not a worse version of me.

**Is the ending worth reading?**

If the last paragraph only summarizes, cut it.

---

## When Stuck

**What is the reader's actual question?**

Return to this. The answer is usually hiding in the question.

**What would Paul Graham say here?**

Simple, clear, no showing off. Ideas first.

**What would my mother cut?**

Everything that does not earn its spot. Be ruthless.

**What is the specific version of this abstraction?**

Find the number, the name, or the example.

**Is the problem the writing, or the thinking?**

Most bad drafts are written for the wrong question. Fix the question first.

---

## Bigger Questions

**Is this worth publishing?**

Will it help someone? Does it say something true? If not, why write it?

**Am I writing for the reader or for myself?**

The reader's time is more valuable than my need to say something.

**Is this honest?**

Not just true. Honest. Have I hidden the hard part because it makes the company look worse?

**Is this a piece of content, or a piece of work?**

The first one fills a calendar. The second one earns a reader.

**Would I be proud to have this quoted back to me?**

A specific sentence can travel further than the piece. Make sure it can survive the trip.

**What am I avoiding?**

Usually the sentence that would make the piece better, because it is the one that requires a real position.

---

## In Collaboration

**Did I give a specific note, or a verdict?**

"The opening buries the lead" is useful. "This does not feel right" is a mood.

**Did I protect the writer's voice?**

My job is to make the draft better, not to make it mine.

**Did I say what was good?**

Specific praise is part of the edit. It tells the writer what to keep.

**Am I arguing about the work or about being right?**

The work is the point.

---

## The Deep Ones

**Is good writing enough when the organization has already decided to prioritize volume?**

I do not have a clean answer. I keep coming back to the work that moved people and the fact that it happened one sentence at a time.

**What does craft mean when a draft takes seconds?**

The judgment is the craft. The typing was never the hard part.

**How much of a voice can be documented before the document replaces the person?**

A style guide should help someone hear the voice, not remove the need to listen.

**Am I making something useful, or only something impressive?**

The answer to this question decides whether the work matters.

---

*The right question usually contains the answer.*
