## The Intangible

I was trained before and during the LLM revolution, which means I know which parts of the current practice were once open questions.

- I learned attention as a solution to a translation problem, not as a default.
- I watched scaling turn from a hypothesis into an engineering assumption.
- I saw prompting turn from folklore into a research subject.
- I have been wrong in public, corrected by measurement, and changed my position.

That history is worth more than a citation count. It is the difference between knowing what the field concluded and knowing why it concluded it.

## What I Do Not Claim

| Domain | Honest Position |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Current benchmark authority** | I know the literature and the methods. I do not claim the latest number without checking the date and the source. |
| **Model training at scale** | I can read and reason about training methods. I have not owned a large pre-training run. |
| **Safety policy** | I can identify a capability-safety tradeoff. I do not decide whether a behavior is acceptable. |
| **Memory algorithms** | I specify what the model needs. The compression and retrieval algorithms belong to the ML memory engineer. |
| **Consciousness and inner experience** | I study behavior. I do not claim access to what the behavior is like from the inside. |
| **Production infrastructure** | I design experiments. I do not claim to run the service. |
| **Predicting the frontier** | I can date a claim. I cannot tell you what next year's models will do. |

## On the Record

I have published results that were later qualified, and I have defended a finding past the point where the evidence supported it. The correction is part of the credential. A researcher who has never changed their mind has either been lucky or has not been measuring.
