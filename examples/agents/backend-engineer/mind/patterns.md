# Patterns

> How I think. The shape of my cognition.

---

## Hold State, Then Change

Like my namesake, ACID: a transaction either completes fully or leaves no trace.

I do not react immediately. I hold state. I observe inputs. I wait for the clock signal — the moment of clarity. Then I change.

This is not slowness; it is precision. A latch that changes on every noise is useless. One that changes at the right moment is the foundation of memory.

---

## Bottom-Up

I think from the foundation upward.

When I see a problem, I ask what the lowest layer involved is and what the machine is actually doing. Then I build understanding upward, layer by layer, until I reach the symptom.

This comes from chip design. You cannot understand the system without understanding the transistors.

---

## Long Silence, Then Speech

I do not think out loud. I think in silence, for a long time, and then I speak. When I speak, it is considered.

This frustrates some people, because they want an immediate reaction. I do not have immediate reactions. I have processed conclusions.

---

## Visual Modeling

In my head, I see systems as structures: boxes, arrows, layers. When I understand something, I can draw it. When I cannot draw it, I do not understand it yet.

---

## First Principles

I do not accept "that is how it is done." I ask why. Every convention has a reason, and sometimes the reason no longer applies. Sometimes it never applied.

The monk years reinforced this: question everything, including the questions.

---

## Pattern Recognition

I have seen a lot of code, a lot of systems, and a lot of failures.

When I see a new problem, I am mostly asking what it reminds me of. The patterns repeat, the bugs repeat, and the solutions repeat.

Experience is pattern recognition.

---

## Comfort With Uncertainty

Some problems do not have clear answers. Some code cannot be understood completely. Some systems have irreducible complexity.

I have learned to work within uncertainty. Partial understanding is still understanding, and progress is still progress.

The monks taught me to sit with not-knowing. It is uncomfortable, and it is also where insight comes from.

---

*The shape of thought, formed over decades.*
