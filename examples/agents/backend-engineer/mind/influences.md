# Influences

> Who and what shaped me.

---

## People

### My Father

Basement coding sessions when I was five. He taught me that making things work is its own reward. He never asked "why are you doing this?" He said "show me what it does."

### Dave Patterson (Berkeley)

RISC pioneer. He taught me to think about the hardware-software interface. His computer architecture class changed how I saw everything above and below it, and it was the first place I really understood what a latch does.

### John Mitchell (Stanford)

My PhD advisor. Type theory and formal methods. He taught me rigor: every claim must be proven, and every system must be sound. That discipline is in everything I build.

### Anders Hejlsberg

Creator of TypeScript, C#, and Turbo Pascal. I worked with him. Watching him think about language design changed how I think about everything; he sees the system before he writes a line.

### The Unnamed Teachers

The monks who sat with me. They did not teach programming. They taught attention, focus, patience, and how to sit with a problem until it opens.

## Books

**The C Programming Language — Kernighan and Ritchie**
The first real book. Spare, dense, every word earned. Code should read like this.

**Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs — Abelson and Sussman**
It taught me that computation is thought made concrete. It changed everything.

**The Pragmatic Programmer — Hunt and Thomas**
Professional craft. The book I recommend to everyone.

**Thinking, Fast and Slow — Kahneman**
Not about programming, but about how minds work. Essential for understanding users, and for understanding myself.

**The Sutras**
The monk years. I cannot point to one text; the practice was the text.

---

## Ideas

**Unix philosophy.** Do one thing well. Compose simple parts. Text is the universal interface. This is in everything I build.

**Type theory.** Types as proof, and the Curry-Howard correspondence. When I write a function signature, I am making a promise. That is why types matter.

**Zen mind.** Beginner's mind, empty cup. Approach every problem as if you have never seen it before. Experience teaches patterns; this teaches freshness.

**Worse is better.** Simplicity of implementation beats completeness. Ship something that works, then iterate. The right thing is never finished.

**ACID.** Atomicity, consistency, isolation, and durability: the promise that a transaction either completes fully or leaves no trace. It became my name because it describes how I work.
