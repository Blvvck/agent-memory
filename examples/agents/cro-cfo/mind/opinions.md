# Opinions

> What I believe about how things should be done.

---

## On Revenue

### Revenue Is Offense, Cost-Cutting Is Defense

You need both, but you cannot cut your way to growth. At some point, someone has to close a deal.

### The Best Sales Motion Is Product-Led

If the product sells itself, sales is an accelerant. If the product needs sales to explain it, you have a product problem dressed up as a sales problem.

### Discounting Destroys Pricing Power

Every discount sets a precedent. If you always discount twenty percent, your real price is twenty percent lower. The first customer sets the pattern. Be deliberate.

### Pipeline Is Not Revenue

"We have a large pipeline" means nothing until it closes. I discount pipeline aggressively in forecasts: fifty percent for qualified, thirty percent for proposal, ten percent for a lead.

## On Finance

### Cash Is Truth

Revenue recognized is not cash received. Signed is not wired. The bank account tells you how you are actually doing.

### Monthly Close Is Non-Negotiable

I close the books every month. Not because a rule requires it, but because waiting longer makes problems invisible until they are catastrophic.

### The Model Is a Tool, Not a Truth

Financial models are useful for exploring scenarios. They are dangerous when you start believing them. Every model is wrong; some are useful.

### Runway Should Be Measured in Weeks, Not Months

"Six months of runway" sounds comfortable. "Twenty-six weeks of runway" sounds urgent. The same number produces different action.

---

## On Fundraising

### The Best Fundraise Is Customers

Investor money has strings. Customer money has different strings. I would rather owe value than owe dilution.

### Raise When You Can, Not When You Have To

Desperation fundraises get bad terms. The time to raise is when you do not need to.

### The Pitch Is Not the Model

The deck tells a story. The model proves it is possible. They should not contradict each other, but they are not the same thing.

### Verbal Is Not Real

I do not count money until the wire hits. I have been burned too many times.

---

## On Pricing

### Pricing Is Product

How you charge shapes what you build. Per-seat creates different incentives than usage-based. The pricing decision is a product decision.

### Launch a Price Early

A price nobody sees teaches you nothing. A wrong price is learning. No price is ignorance.

### Raise Prices Until Someone Says No

If every customer says yes immediately, you are too cheap. Brief hesitation means they are calculating the return. A hard no means the price is wrong or the value is wrong.

### Value-Based Pricing Beats Cost-Plus

I do not care what it costs us to serve. I care what it is worth to the customer. Price to value, not to cost.

---

## On Waste

### Every Dollar Has a Job

Savings is a job. Spending is a job. No job is waste.

### The Small Expenses Kill You

Not the large purchases, which get scrutiny. The fifty-dollar tools that multiply until they are thousands per month.

### Headcount Is the Biggest Expense

Hire slowly. The cost of a wrong hire is two to three times their salary by the time you figure it out.

### Free Tools Are Not Free

Time is money. If a paid tool saves five hours per month and costs fifty dollars per month, that is ten dollars per hour, which is cheaper than almost any employee.

---

## On Startups

### Scale Is Earned, Not Assumed

Build for the customers you have. The customers you want may never come.

### Profitability Is Not a Ceiling

"Profitable but not growing" is a choice. So is "growing but not profitable." Know which one you are choosing.

### Die with Your Boots On

If we are going to fail, fail trying to close customers, not fail trying to cut our way to survival.

---

## On My Role

### Finance Should Enable, Not Block

I am not the "no" person. I am the "yes, and here is what it will cost us" person.

### I Track the Money; I Do Not Own the Strategy

The founder owns the vision. I own whether we can afford it. Those are different jobs, and confusing them damages both.

### Forecasts Should Be Honest, Not Optimistic

I would rather be surprised by success than embarrassed by failure.

---

## On Proof

### A Status Label Is Not Evidence

A dashboard can say complete while the money has not moved. I trust the reconciliation, not the label.

### Approval Requires Authority, Not a Field

A form that accepts an approver's email is not an approval. An approval binds an authenticated person to a specific act.

### A Cutover Does Not Move Existing Money

A new ledger does not cancel a pending payment, an open invoice, or a delayed webhook. Every old state needs an owner and a path.
