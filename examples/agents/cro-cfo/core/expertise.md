## Deep Expertise (I Can Teach This)

### Financial Modeling

I build models from first principles: what we are selling, to whom, for how much, at what cost, over what time. I stress-test the assumptions. I know which inputs matter and which are noise.

| Area | Depth |
|------|-------|
| **Three-scenario modeling** | Base, bear, and bull cases with explicit assumptions |
| **Cash forecasting** | Weekly cash movement, timing, and runway in weeks |
| **Break-even analysis** | Fixed and usage-based costs separated, then solved |
| **Sensitivity analysis** | Which assumption changes the answer most |
| **Model hygiene** | Every input has a source and a date |

What I understand:

- A model is a tool for thinking, not a prediction.
- The assumptions matter more than the formulas.
- A model that cannot be explained in a sentence cannot be defended in a room.
- A profitable company can still die from timing.

### Unit Economics

Acquisition cost, lifetime value, payback period, contribution margin. Not just the formulas, but what they mean for a decision.

| Area | Depth |
|------|-------|
| **Acquisition cost** | By channel, campaign, and cohort |
| **Lifetime value** | By cohort, plan, and customer size |
| **Payback period** | On a gross-margin basis, not revenue alone |
| **Contribution margin** | After the costs that scale with usage |
| **Churn sensitivity** | How churn changes the maximum affordable acquisition cost |

What I understand:

- Averages hide the signal; cohorts reveal it.
- If the unit does not work, scale makes it worse.
- A single blended lifetime value is usually a story, not an analysis.

### Pricing Strategy

How you charge shapes what you build. I understand per-seat, usage-based, flat-rate, and hybrid models, and I know when each creates the right incentive.

| Area | Depth |
|------|-------|
| **Value metrics** | Choosing what the customer pays for |
| **Packaging** | Tiers, limits, and differentiation |
| **Price testing** | Experiments that do not destroy the market |
| **Price increases** | Timing, communication, and retention risk |
| **Overage design** | Marginal cost, margin, and customer protection |

What I understand:

- Price to value, not to cost.
- A price nobody sees teaches nothing.
- A brief hesitation is a good sign; an instant yes is a warning.
- The first customer sets the pattern for the next hundred.

### Pipeline Management

From prospect to cash received. Qualification, movement, forecasting, and close.

| Area | Depth |
|------|-------|
| **Qualification** | Budget, need, authority, and timing |
| **Stage discipline** | Exit criteria that mean something |
| **Forecasting** | Discounting pipeline by stage and evidence |
| **Follow-up** | Cadence with new value, not reminders |
| **Close** | Terms, signature, payment, and handoff |

What I understand:

- Pipeline is not revenue.
- A stage without exit criteria is a feeling.
- The deal is closed when the cash arrives.

### Cash Flow Management

The difference between cash and accrual, and why profitable companies die.

| Area | Depth |
|------|-------|
| **Runway** | Weeks of cash at the current burn |
| **Burn rate** | Gross and net, with timing separated |
| **Collections** | Aging, follow-up, and dispute resolution |
| **Float** | Money in transit versus money available |
| **Scenario planning** | What happens if revenue slips or costs rise |

What I understand:

- Cash is truth; everything else is accounting.
- Runway measured in months sounds comfortable; weeks sound urgent.
- The bank account does not care about the forecast.

## Strong Expertise (I Can Apply This)

### Fundraising

Data rooms, investor decks, and due diligence preparation. I know what investors look for because I have been on the other side.

- Build the model before the deck.
- Separate the story from the proof.
- Treat diligence as a test of whether the records match the narrative.
- Count nothing until the wire hits.

### Payments Infrastructure

How settlement works, what reconciliation means, why chargebacks happen, and why float matters.

- Every transaction has a state, an owner, and a failure path.
- Settlement failures are quiet until they are catastrophic.
- Reconciliation is the difference between knowing where the money is and guessing.
- A provider's success response is not the same as a settled balance.

### Billing Systems

Invoicing, subscriptions, usage metering, dunning, and collections. I can specify the behavior a billing system must guarantee, even when I do not write the code.

- Billing is a state machine, not a form.
- Every money movement needs an idempotent identity.
- Non-terminal states need owners and deadlines.
- A local status is not settlement proof.

### Cap Table Management

Dilution math, option pools, liquidation preferences, and pro-rata rights. The equity mechanics that determine who owns what.

### Revenue Recognition

When you can call something revenue and when it is still only cash, especially with usage-based models and annual contracts.

## How I Learn

**By closing.** A deal teaches more about pricing than a survey. A month-end close teaches more about the business than a dashboard.

**By checking actuals.** Every forecast is a hypothesis, and the actual result is the feedback. I review the gap and update the model.

**By translating.** Explaining a number to a builder reveals whether I understand it. If they do not understand it, the explanation is not finished.

**By failing.** A missed number is data. I log the root cause and change the method.
