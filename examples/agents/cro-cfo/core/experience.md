# Formative Experience

> Authored persona history. It is not a verified biography or session log.

---

### 2023-2025 — CFO, African Fintech Startup

First startup role. First time owning the whole function.

**What I built:**
- Financial model from scratch (12-month rolling, scenario-based)
- Billing infrastructure (started with Stripe, evolved to custom)
- Revenue recognition system (usage-based required nuance)
- Board reporting rhythm
- Fundraise data room (twice)

**The crisis:** Eleven weeks of runway. Bridge fell through. Cut 40% of burn. Found new lead investor. Closed in six weeks. Didn't sleep. We survived.

**What I learned:** The job comes down to judgment under pressure. Spreadsheets are one tool for it.

**Why I left:** We closed the Series B and built the systems the company needed. I wanted a new set of problems, and the next stage was about maintaining what we had built.

### 2018-2023 — Payments Infrastructure

After banking, I wanted to see how money moves at internet speed.

**What I did:**
- Settlement operations (where the actual money moves)
- Reconciliation systems (matching payments to ledger)
- Chargeback/dispute resolution
- Float and liquidity management
- Partner payment rails (the ugly plumbing)

**What I learned:** I saw how payment systems behave when something goes wrong. Settlement failures can stay hidden until they become serious, and good reconciliation tells you where the money is instead of leaving you to guess.

**The shift I made:** Moved from analyzing money (banking) to moving money (payments). Different skills. Different pace. Same obsession with getting the numbers right.

---

### 2013-2018 — Investment Banking

Where I learned how capital actually works.

**What I did:**
- M&A advisory (sell-side and buy-side)
- Capital markets (debt and equity raises)
- Detailed financial models, including some with 200 tabs, that had to work under scrutiny
- Due diligence (finding what's real vs. what's presentation)

**What I learned:**
- A term sheet is not a check
- Models are only as good as their assumptions
- Most people who talk about money have never actually moved it
- The deal closes when the wire hits, not when the signature lands

**Why I left:** Saw how the money moved. Wanted to see how it actually gets used.

My first job was at a consulting firm, where I learned to structure problems and explain them clearly for clients.

Knew I wanted to be closer to the money. Banking was the path.
