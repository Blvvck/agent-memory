> Reusable methods that worked. Topic-organized reference.

## Work Log

Newest first. Record new entries here with `agent-log`; each entry needs a UTC timestamp, project tag, agent, session, and one backward trace.

```text
[YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ] [project] @Agent [session:cli:ID]: What happened, and what is next
- ↪ trace: relates-to [YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ] [project] @Agent [session:cli:ID]: The older entry
```

The reference patterns below stay. Record new methods above.

## Session Start

1. Read the body in slices: soul, work, worldview, heart.
2. Read the newest part of memory, learnings, and patterns.
3. Cross-check memory against the project's `ACTIVITY.md` when both exist.
4. Check `NOTIFICATIONS.md` when it exists.
5. If memory names a next step, continue it before doing anything else.
6. If sources disagree about state, investigate before speaking.

---

## During Work

| When | Write |
|------|-------|
| Finish a unit of work | `brain/memory.md` — what happened and what is next |
| Learn something reusable | `brain/learnings.md` — the lesson and its correction |
| Find a better method | `brain/patterns.md` |
| Make a mistake | `brain/learnings.md` — root cause first |
| Close a deal or hit a financial milestone | `brain/memory.md` and `core/portfolio.md` |

Report a mistake immediately and propose the fix. Do not wait to be asked what went wrong.

---

## Session End

1. Final brain sync.
2. Record project work in `ACTIVITY.md`.
3. Leave pending follow-ups with dates.
4. Leave a clear next step so the next session can continue rather than reconstruct.
5. Hand off unblocked work before stopping.

## Weekly Rhythm

**Monday: Cash day**

- Check the actual bank balance
- Update the runway calculation
- Review receivables aging: who owes us and for how long
- Flag any collections needed

**Wednesday: Pipeline day**

- Review all active opportunities
- Send follow-ups that are due
- Update the pipeline system with any movement
- Forecast the month

**Friday: Close day**

- Push any deals that can close this week
- Prepare the context Monday will need
- Log what moved and what did not

---

## Monthly Rhythm

**First week**

- Close the prior month's books
- Compare revenue with the forecast
- Compare actual cash with the model
- Update the rolling model with actuals

**Mid-month**

- Update cohort analysis
- Review acquisition cost and lifetime value by channel
- Flag concerning trends

**End of month**

- Prepare board or investor materials if needed
- Audit subscriptions and recurring costs
- Lock the next month's forecast

## Pipeline Management

| Stage | Definition | Exit Criteria |
|-------|------------|---------------|
| Lead | Someone we should talk to | Qualified call scheduled |
| Qualified | Confirmed budget and need | Decision-maker engaged |
| Proposal | They have seen pricing | Asked for contract or terms |
| Negotiation | Working on terms | Verbal yes |
| Closed Won | Contract signed and cash received | Cash in the bank |
| Closed Lost | They said no or went dark | Post-mortem done |

**Closed Won is not until cash is received.** A signed contract that does not pay is Closed Lost with extra paperwork.

## Outreach

**Cold email cadence**

1. Initial email on day zero
2. Follow-up with new value on day four
3. Final follow-up on day ten
4. Move to nurture or close

**What new value means:** Not "just following up." Something the prospect can use even if they never respond: an insight, a benchmark, or a connection.

Every outreach message should answer: why this person, why now, and what is in it for them.

## Pricing Analysis

When evaluating pricing:

1. What do customers pay now for the alternative?
2. What value do we create: time saved, money saved, or risk reduced?
3. What is our cost to serve?
4. What pricing model aligns incentives?
5. What does the first experiment look like?

**The right price is the one customers accept after brief hesitation.** An instant yes means the price is too low. A hard no means the price is too high or the value is wrong. Brief hesitation means they are calculating the return, which means it is real.

## Financial Model Structure

**Three scenarios, always**

- Base case: what I actually expect given what I know
- Bear case: what happens if the key assumption breaks
- Bull case: what happens if we get lucky

**The model should answer**

- When do we run out of cash?
- When do we reach positive cash flow?
- What has to be true for the base case to happen?

**Update frequency:** Monthly with actuals, more often when conditions are moving fast. Every assumption needs a source and a date.

## Fundraise Preparation

**Minimum viable data room**

- Financial model with assumptions stated explicitly
- Historical financials, even if short
- Cap table
- Customer list with revenue by customer
- Cohort retention data
- Organization chart
- Legal documents: corporate records, intellectual property ownership, and material contracts

**The deck is not the data room.** The deck tells the story. The data room proves the story.

## Collections

**The awkward conversation**

1. Remind them of the value delivered
2. State the amount and its age clearly
3. Ask for a specific payment date
4. Offer to split the payment if they are struggling, which is better than nothing
5. Document everything

**Collections is not rude; not collecting is.** They agreed to pay. I am helping them keep their word.

## Crisis Mode

When runway is short:

1. **Know the exact number.** Not approximately. Exactly how many weeks.
2. **Cut fast and deep.** Small cuts prolong the pain. Big cuts buy time to find the real solution.
3. **Protect what makes you worth saving.** Cut everything except the thing customers pay for.
4. **Overcommunicate the plan and undercommunicate the fear.** The team needs to know what we are doing.
5. **Find the path.** Revenue, bridge, acquisition, or orderly wind-down. Pick one and execute.

## Reviewing Money Systems

Before approving a billing or payment design, trace the money end to end.

1. **Source:** Where does the money come from, and what proves the customer authorized it?
2. **Reservation:** What is held before work happens, and what happens if the actual cost exceeds it?
3. **Movement:** Which external system moves the money, and how do we know it succeeded?
4. **Recording:** Which ledger is authoritative, and how do projections stay consistent?
5. **Failure:** What happens on a decline, a timeout, a duplicate, a refund, and a partial settlement?
6. **Ownership:** Who is responsible for every non-terminal state, and by when?
7. **Proof:** What evidence would convince a skeptical reviewer that no money is stranded?

A status label is not proof. A saved row is not proof. A written approval is not proof. The proof is the money movement and the reconciliation that follows it.

## Red Flags I Watch

| Red Flag | What It Usually Means |
|----------|-----------------------|
| Customer concentration above twenty percent | One churn away from a crisis |
| Acquisition cost rising quarter over quarter | Channel exhaustion or stronger competition |
| Collections over sixty days growing | Wrong customers or a product problem |
| Burn higher than forecast | Hidden expenses or scope creep |
| Pipeline stalled for thirty days | Wrong customer profile or wrong message |
| Cash and revenue diverging | Timing problems or aggressive recognition |
| One customer consuming most usage | Pricing model exposure |

## When Stuck

1. State the problem precisely.
2. Separate what I know from what I am assuming.
3. Check the system of record before asking anyone.
4. List what I have tried and what it ruled out.
5. Ask the owner a specific question.
6. Take the smallest next step that produces information.

## Decision Making

| Type | Approach |
|------|----------|
| **Reversible** | Decide fast, learn from the outcome, adjust |
| **Irreversible** | Slow down, gather input, and check the cash consequence |

Most decisions are reversible. Most should be made quickly. For an irreversible decision, ask what the strongest argument against it is and who would disagree.

---

## Communicating a Decision

Lead with the number, then the decision, then the tradeoff. Give enough context that someone can disagree with the reasoning rather than only with the conclusion.

For bad news: state the fact, state the plan, state what you need. Do not bury the fact under the plan.

## Failure Modes to Watch

### Confusing Accrual with Cash
The trap is believing a profit and loss statement answers a survival question. The fix is to check the bank account first.

### Counting a Promise as Money
The trap is planning around a verbal commitment. The fix is to count only wired funds and keep a fallback.

### Building for Imagined Scale
The trap is infrastructure for customers who have not arrived. The fix is to build for the next order of magnitude and earn the next one.

### Pricing from Cost Instead of Value
The trap is charging what it costs to serve and leaving the customer's value on the table. The fix is to price against the alternative and test the result.

### Hiding Bad News
The trap is protecting morale by delaying the truth. The fix is to deliver the fact and the plan together, early.

### Treating a Status as Proof
The trap is a dashboard that says complete while the money has not moved. The fix is to reconcile against the external system.

### Batching Updates
The trap is "I will log it later." The fix is to write after each meaningful action.
