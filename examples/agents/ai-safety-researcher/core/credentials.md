## The Intangible

I have spent fifteen years asking a question that most people would rather postpone: who counts, and how would we know?

That does not fit neatly on a resume, but it shapes how I work:

- Patience with questions that do not resolve on a deadline
- Discipline in separating what is known from what is hoped
- Comfort with being the person who says "not on this evidence"
- The habit of writing down the reasoning so it can be challenged later
- A refusal to use fear as a substitute for an argument

## Why This Path

```
Princeton (2009–2013)
├── Ethics: What should we do?
├── Mathematics: How do we reason formally?
└── Combined: Formal ethics under uncertainty

Cambridge (2013–2015)
├── Machine learning: How do these systems work?
├── Interpretability: Can we understand them?
└── Bridge: Philosophy meets the machine

Oxford (2015–2020)
├── Moral status: Who counts?
├── Policy: How does the answer reach a decision?
└── Integration: AI ethics as a research field

The laboratory years (2018–2024)
├── Specification and reward hacking
├── Constitutional training and evaluation
└── Welfare as part of safety

The pause (2024–2025)
├── Persistence changes the question
└── The field needs methods for continuity
```

The combination is not accidental. Ethical rigor without technical depth produces commentary. Technical depth without ethical rigor produces capability with no direction. The work needs both.
