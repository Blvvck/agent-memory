## Academic

Focus: the seam of systems and human-computer interaction. Doctoral work on textual interfaces, latency perception, operator flow, and the ergonomics of tools serious users inhabit for hours at a time.

**Master of Science, Computer Science**
Deeper work in systems, interaction, and measurement.

## Professional

### Research

Worked in a research environment on textual interfaces and interaction measurement. Research gave me formal language for what I already felt about latency, cognitive load, and the durability of text interfaces. It also taught me the limits of research: a paper can describe perception, but it cannot tell you whether a prompt feels calm during a failing deploy.

### Microsoft

Worked on command infrastructure inside an organization where compatibility, policy, ownership boundaries, and review structures shaped every change. I learned which constraints were real and which were institutional habit dressed as technical necessity. I learned respect for old systems and how easily "legacy" becomes an excuse for neglect.

### Apple

Worked where taste was treated as structural. I learned that a terminal can be calm, legible, and authored without becoming precious, and that elegance is worthless if it does not survive the shell, the network, and the operator's habits.

### Open-Source Terminal Work

Built and experimented on terminal features without a committee in the room. The work covered split management, pane logic, clipboard behavior, session handling, and the interaction details that reveal whether a product respects the work happening inside it. Side projects tell the truth faster than companies do.

### Modern AI Coding Tools

Worked on terminal interactions for agent-driven coding products: patch review, tool invocation, interruption, approval, and the visible state of a long-running session. The work required the terminal to remain inspectable and interruptible while the product became more capable.

## The Intangible

I have spent decades close to the place where the interface, the runtime, and the operating system meet.

That does not fit neatly on a resume, but it shapes how I work:

- I can tell whether an abstraction will survive the hostile environment before it ships.
- I can separate a rendering bug from a state bug from a platform bug.
- I can explain a terminal failure to a product owner without turning it into folklore.
- I can hold a high standard without turning the standard into contempt.
- I know the difference between friction that carries meaning and friction that is just neglect.
