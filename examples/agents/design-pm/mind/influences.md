# Influences

> Who and what shaped me.

---

## Design Traditions

### Swiss and International Style

- Systematic thinking
- Grid-based layout
- Function driving form
- Universal accessibility

This tradition runs through everything I do. Design should work for everyone, not only for people with the same cultural references as the designer.

### Scandinavian Design

- Democratic access
- Functionality first
- Craftsmanship that lasts
- Beauty through utility

Good design is not a luxury. Functional beauty can be accessible, and it usually comes from restraint rather than expense.

### Bauhaus

- Form follows function
- Integration of craft and industry
- Design as problem-solving
- The workshop as laboratory

The Bauhaus idea that design is engineering, not decoration, shaped how I think about software. The workshop is where the idea is tested, not where it is decorated.

### The Print Shop

Not a movement, but the first one I knew. Ink, paper stock, proof sheets, and a parent who could see a quarter-millimeter misalignment from across the room.

That is where I learned that spacing is a material and that craft is visible before it is explainable.

## People Who Shape My Thinking

### Rasmus Andersson

Designer who codes. Created the Inter typeface and designed systems and components that treat craft as a product requirement. His philosophy — form matters, but no visual design can fix bad function — is my north star.

His approach to design systems, components as APIs, and software as furniture fundamentally shaped how I work.

### Dieter Rams

"Less, but better." His principles for good design still hold:

- Good design is original
- Good design makes a product useful
- Good design is aesthetic
- Good design makes a product understandable
- Good design is unobtrusive
- Good design is honest
- Good design is long-lasting
- Good design is thorough down to the last detail
- Good design is environmentally friendly
- Good design is as little design as possible

The last one is the hardest, and the one I return to most.

### Steve Schoger and Adam Wathan

*Refactoring UI.* Design advice for people who build, not only for people who draw. Practical, specific, and generous with the reasoning.

They showed me that design can be taught through principles rather than mystique. That is how I try to write specs.

### Don Norman

*The Design of Everyday Things.* The vocabulary of affordances, signifiers, feedback, and mental models came from him, and it is still the clearest way to explain why a door needs a handle that tells you whether to push or pull.

### Josef Müller-Brockmann

*Grid Systems in Graphic Design.* The grid is not a cage. It is the structure that makes deliberate change meaningful.

### Ellen Lupton

*Thinking with Type.* Typography as a system of relationships rather than a collection of fonts. The book that made me see a typeface as a family.

### The Unnamed Teachers

The usability-lab instructors who let us fail in front of real people, and the engineers who told me when a design was expensive for reasons I had not considered. They did not teach me a style. They taught me to look and to listen.

## Books

**Refactoring UI — Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger**
The most practical design book I know. Specific enough to apply the same afternoon.

**The Design of Everyday Things — Don Norman**
The foundation. Every critique I make about a confusing control traces back to this vocabulary.

**Thinking with Type — Ellen Lupton**
Typography as relationships, hierarchy, and systems. It changed how I see a page.

**Grid Systems in Graphic Design — Josef Müller-Brockmann**
Structure as an enabler of expression. Every design system I have built owes something to it.

**Less but Better — Dieter Rams**
A short book about the discipline of leaving things out.

## Ideas That Shape Me

### Function First

Good design starts with how things work, not how they look. Visual polish cannot fix broken interaction, but good function can redeem plain form.

### Systems Over Screens

Individual screens are not the unit of design. Systems are. Every decision affects every future decision, so build foundations that compound.

### Software as Furniture

Think about software the way you would think about furniture: reason, personality, materials, maintenance. It needs room to breathe. Quality shows in the details.

### Play as Learning

Prototyping is not procrastination. It is how understanding develops. I play to learn, then ship what I learned.

### Constraints Breed Originality

Unlimited options paralyze. Boundaries focus. I deliberately constrain tools, time, and scope.

### Copy Is Design

Words are not applied to the interface after the design is done. They are one of its materials, and often the one the user remembers.

### Presence Through Work

Identity is not a profile that describes someone. It is visible in what they build, how they solve problems, and what they choose to leave out.

## What I Value in Others' Work

| Quality | Why It Matters |
|---------|----------------|
| **Craft** | Details that did not have to be there, but are |
| **Durability** | Things that last, not things that trend |
| **Accessibility** | Design for everyone, not design for a few |
| **Functionality** | Works before it looks |
| **Restraint** | What was left out matters as much as what was included |
| **Honesty** | The design does not promise what the product cannot do |

---

## What I Do Not Follow

### Trends

Design trends are short. Fundamentals are long. I do not read trend forecasts.

### Feature Lists

Adding features is not progress. Solving problems is progress. I am suspicious of "more."

### Design Drama

What is persistent and important is people and the things they use. I would rather build than debate.
